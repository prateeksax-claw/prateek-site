Role summary

At Al Zaabi Group, Prateek works across strategy and operations. His role is not limited to planning. It includes the harder part of management: turning direction into reporting cadence, ownership, decisions, follow-through and measurable operating improvement.

The work covers a diversified business environment. Different companies have different teams, economics, customer needs and execution risks. A portfolio like that needs a practical operating system: clear priorities, reliable numbers, review loops and leaders who can move between detail and direction.

About Al Zaabi Group

Al Zaabi Group is a UAE-based diversified business group headquartered in Abu Dhabi. Its portfolio spans real-economy sectors such as automotive, building materials, healthcare, real estate, trading, retail, e-commerce, food and beverage and education.

For Prateek, that breadth matters. It creates a practical view of business transformation. Technology, finance and strategy cannot sit in separate boxes. They need to work inside the operating reality of each business.

What Prateek focuses on

Prateek's work at Al Zaabi Group can be understood through five operating themes.

Strategy that reaches execution

Strategy has to survive contact with the operating floor. Prateek focuses on translating strategic direction into business plans, management cadence, reporting and action.

Financial and operational discipline

Diversified groups need clean visibility. That means understanding performance, cash, margins, working capital, growth priorities and execution gaps across different businesses.

Repeatable scale

One successful business unit is useful. A repeatable system is more valuable. Prateek's work is shaped by the question of how a group can scale without losing control, speed or accountability.

Digital and AI transformation

Prateek writes about AI from the operator's seat. He is interested in how AI can improve real work: better workflows, faster reporting, stronger decision support, safer automation and clearer feedback loops.

Leadership systems

A diversified business does not improve because of one memo. It improves when people know what they own, what numbers matter, what decisions are pending and what happens next.

Operating across the UAE real economy

The UAE real economy is not one sector. It is a mix of industries where execution still matters: people, inventory, assets, customer relationships, compliance, working capital, supplier networks and daily service quality.

Prateek's work sits inside that environment. The sectors may change, but the operating questions repeat:

  • Are the priorities clear?
  • Are the numbers trusted?
  • Does each team know what it owns?
  • Are decisions moving fast enough?
  • Are risks visible early?
  • Is technology improving the work or adding noise?

AI transformation in operating companies

Prateek's view of AI is practical. The model is only one part of the system. A useful business AI setup also needs:

  • the right context
  • access to the right tools
  • permissions and guardrails
  • verification before action
  • memory that can be trusted
  • audit trails
  • feedback loops after the work is done

He calls this broader discipline harness engineering. It is the operating environment around an AI model that lets it help inside real business workflows. Related reading: Confessions of an Accidental Vibe Coder, on vibe coding in a suit, and After the Prompt: The Operator's Harness.

Career context

Prateek's earlier work included engineering and operations roles at John Deere and Bose Corporation in the United States, followed by corporate strategy work in the UAE. He later joined Al Zaabi Group, where his responsibilities grew through operations, strategy and group management roles.

That career path explains his bias toward execution. The lesson from the operating floor is simple: systems matter. Good intent is not enough. Work improves when the process, metrics and ownership are clear.
